From a Diner Booth to a $5 Trillion Empire: How Nvidia Changed the Future of Technology.
By Alkesh Gupta • 8 min read • July 2026

Every tech giant has an origin story. Apple had a garage; Facebook had a Harvard dorm room. Nvidia's story begins with bottomless coffee and Grand Slam breakfasts inside a Denny's diner.

In 1993, Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem met at a Denny's in East San Jose. With only $600 in startup capital, they founded Nvidia—an idea that would eventually become the world's first $5 trillion company.

The Video Game Catalyst

The founders realized that traditional CPUs could never deliver the realistic 3D graphics gamers wanted. Their answer was the Graphics Processing Unit (GPU), capable of performing thousands of mathematical calculations simultaneously.

For years, gamers unknowingly financed Nvidia's research through their demand for increasingly realistic graphics.

"The same chip that powered video games would later become the engine behind artificial intelligence."

The CUDA Revolution

Everything changed in 2006 with CUDA. Instead of limiting GPUs to graphics, Nvidia allowed researchers to use them for scientific computing, medical research, weather simulations and deep learning.

Powering the AI Boom

When ChatGPT and generative AI exploded, every technology company suddenly needed enormous computing power. Companies rushed to purchase Nvidia H100 and Blackwell GPUs, turning Nvidia into the backbone of modern AI infrastructure.
